K-theory

The following is an exerpt from an interview with Thomas Nikolaus. Some small digressions and misphrasins have been edited.

When I learned K-theory, I went through the standard literature, and you see the plus construction, and you attach cells, and then you are asking "Why? Why does one do that?" Sometimes in math you can't explain that—there's some construction you do because it works—but most of the time I feel you can give a deeper meaning. And in the case of K-theory it's in my opinion pretty obvious: you define K₀ as a group completion, so you should somehow define the higher K-theory also as a group completion. It just feels most natural. Actually, if you read Quillen's old papers, he has realised that completely. Of course, back in the day the technology wasn't there, to not only to give that definition but to work with it. But I always think one should have the right definition. Even if that means it becomes more technical eventually to work with it, I would always strive to give the correct definition, because this is what somehow is most important: "this is the object defined like that, it has this property and that pins it down." And then maybe eventually you have to invest more into the technique to work with it; that's basically what is done these days in homotopy theory and higher category theory.
